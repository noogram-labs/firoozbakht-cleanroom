# L9 — Cramér's model theorem: `limsup = 1` **for the model**, suggested for the primes

**Kind:** theorem (about a random model) + a suggestion (about primes)
**Verdict:** **PROVEN about the model. NOT a proven or even a formally conjectured statement about
the primes** — the source offers it as a suggestion, and this distinction is load-bearing.
**Rests on:** `cramer1936order` (L0, scanned original, pages read as images):
- **p. 24, eq. (4)**, verbatim: *"It is suggested that the true maximum order of `p_{n+1} − p_n`
  should be equal to `(log p_n)²`, so that we should be able to replace (1) and (3) by …
  `p_{n+1} − p_n = O((log p_n)²)`."*
- **p. 27, final display**, verbatim: *"Combining these two results, we obtain the following
  theorem: **With a probability = 1, the relation** `limsup_{n→∞} (P_{n+1} − P_n)/(log P_n)² = 1`"*
- **pp. 26–27** — the urn model: urns `U_n` with white-ball probability `1/log n`; `P_n` is the
  index of the urn giving the `n`-th white ball.

---

## Statement, split into its two halves

**(a) Theorem, proved, about the model.** For the random sequence `P_n` generated by the urn
model, `limsup (P_{n+1} − P_n)/(log P_n)² = 1` with probability 1.

**(b) Suggestion, about the primes.** That the true maximum order of `g_n` "should be equal to
`(log p_n)²`" — offered as a suggestion, in Cramér's own word.

## Role in the proof-obligation tree

`L9` is the *baseline model* against which **L10** is a correction. On its own it is compatible
with `F`: `limsup c_n = 1` and `F ⟹ limsup c_n ≤ 1` (**L3**) sit together, tightly, with `F`
forcing the `limsup` to sit exactly at its predicted value from below. **It is L10, not L9, that
contradicts Firoozbakht.** Getting this right is the difference between "the standard model
agrees with the conjecture at the boundary" and "the standard model refutes it".

## Hazards — and this is the most-misquoted card in the set

1. **"Cramér conjectured `limsup g_n/log²p_n = 1`" is a compression the source does not literally
   support.** Cramér *proved* it about `P_n` and *suggested* the analogue for `p_n`. Granville
   frames it the same way (`granville1995cramer` preprint p. 10: *"So what Cramér seems to be
   suggesting…"*, and p. 10 eq. (14): *"This statement (or the weaker `O(log²x)`) is known as
   'Cramér's Conjecture.'"*). **The defensible sentence is: "Cramér's model yields `limsup = 1`
   with probability 1, which he offered as a suggestion for the primes."**
   (`source-ledger.md` §2.2, §4.6.)
2. **The `O(log²)` form and the `∼ log²` form are different statements** and Granville explicitly
   distinguishes them. `F` implies the first (**L3**). It is *incompatible* with the second only
   once Granville's correction is applied (**L10**).
3. The `cramer1936order` scan has **no text layer**. The four cited pages were read as images. A
   later leg re-checking a quotation must re-read the images, not grep the PDF.
4. `decompose` §3.3/§4.4 phrase this as a Cramér conjecture about primes. Tighten before
   publication. (`source-ledger.md` §4.6.)

## Declared gap

None on the source. The gap is entirely in how the result is usually *reported*, which is why the
card exists.
